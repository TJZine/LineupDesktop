## Item 1. Canvas and size-class policy (MD-1)

**Problem.** The 1920×1080 canvas is either microscopic (B1) or scaled to 0.8
into a canvas far narrower than any approved composition (B2). Controls drawn at
40–56 canvas units become 32–45 dp, below the 48 dp touch minimum.

**Options rendered** (`sheets/item1-scale-0.8-0.9-1.0.png`,
`sheets/item1-touch-audit.png`, `audit/summary.md`):

| Handheld scale | Body / small text | Canvas at 360 / 412 dp wide | Sampled targets under 48 dp (412×915) | Headings breaking mid-word |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.8 (today's desktop floor) | 14.4 / 11.2 dp | 450 / 515 cu | 35 of 59 | at text 2.0 ("Appearan / ce") |
| **0.9 (recommended)** | **16.2 / 12.6 dp** | **400 / 458 cu** | **15 of 59** | **at text 2.0 ("sc / heduled", "Appeara / nce")** |
| 1.0 | 18 / 14 dp | 360 / 412 cu | 3 of 58 | already at text 1.0 on 360 dp ("Appearanc / e") |

**Layout exceptions are not a differentiator.** Every exception thrown in the
handheld runs, at all three scales and at text 1.0, 1.3 and 2.0, comes from the
current Guide (`guide_view.dart:2969` schedule status, `:2834`, `:3272`,
`:3700`). The Guide stays mounted beneath Settings and the Player, so its
overflow also fails those scenes. Onboarding, Settings and the Player OSD throw
nothing of their own up to text 2.0 at 0.9 (`sheets/item1-text-scale.png`). The
Guide is redesigned in part 2, item 3. An earlier version of this draft
attributed those failures to the other surfaces; that was wrong (see Review).

**Recommendation.**

- **1a. Two composition classes, chosen by the current window.**
  - **Wide:** window width ≥ 1280 dp *and* height ≥ 720 dp. It uses the
    approved desktop composition, unchanged in structure, with the desktop scale
    rule (`max(0.8, min(w/1920, h/1080))`). A 1280×800 tablet in landscape is
    exactly the approved desktop minimum.
  - **Handheld:** every other window: phones, portrait tablets, small
    landscape tablets, foldables, split-screen. It uses the handheld
    compositions defined per surface in part 2.
  - Material's 600/840 dp breakpoints remain available *inside* the Handheld
    class: a surface may add a second pane when the window is ≥ 600 dp wide
    (canvas ≥ 667 cu). Each surface states this in part 2. No third global
    composition.
  - The Handheld canvas therefore spans 400×889 cu (360×800 phone) up to
    1421×889 cu (a 1279×800 landscape window) and 889×1422 cu (800×1280
    portrait). Part 2 compositions must cover short, wide Handheld windows
    (960×600 → 1067×667 cu) as well as tall ones.
- **1b. Handheld scale is a fixed 0.9.** The canvas is the window divided by 0.9
  (360×800 → 400×889 cu; 412×915 → 458×1017; 600×960 → 667×1067; 690×840 →
  767×933; 800×1280 → 889×1422; 960×600 → 1067×667; 915×412 → 1017×458).
  - The type tokens are unchanged (the system preserves production type sizes),
    so body text is 16.2 dp: Android's standard body size.
  - Wide keeps the desktop rule so the approved composition is pixel-for-pixel
    the desktop one.
  - Rotating a 1280×800 tablet moves it between Wide (0.8) and Handheld (0.9):
    text becomes 12.5 % larger in portrait. This is accepted, because the
    composition changes at the same moment.
- **1c. Touch targets: 48×48 dp minimum hit region on handheld, in both
  classes.**
  - That is 54 cu at 0.9 and 60 cu at 0.8. Visual sizes stay as approved.
  - Hit regions expand symmetrically around the visual control, never
    overlapping a neighbor's. Where neighbors are closer than the expansion,
    the gap is split evenly and the surface spec in part 2 adds spacing.
  - Measured at 0.9, the remaining shortfalls are all the 48 cu controls: OSD
    action chips (43 dp), icon buttons (43 dp), text back links (43 dp tall), and
    the Mini Guide steppers.
  - Exemptions must be named in the surface item, never implied. The expected
    ones are Guide program cells for programs shorter than ~15 minutes (item 3
    defines tap resolution) and the progress lane (item 4 defines drag).
- **1d. Text scale.** No global clamp; Android 14+ nonlinear scaling passes
  through Flutter's `TextScaler` unchanged. Layouts reflow (wrap, rows grow,
  panels scroll). A surface may clamp a fixed-geometry element (Guide cells, OSD
  chips, channel bug) to at most 1.3 with a local
  `MediaQuery.withClampedTextScaling`. The part 2 item names each clamp.
  - **No word ever breaks mid-word.** At text 2.0, 0.8 and 0.9 both split
    large headings inside a word. Headline and display roles (≥ 28 cu) wrap
    only at word boundaries. A single word that still cannot fit the line
    shrinks to fit, never below its text-1.0 size, instead of breaking.
  - Font scale above 1.3 exists only on Android 14+, where the platform scales
    large text nonlinearly (less than body text). Acceptance renders at 1.3 and
    2.0 must apply Android's nonlinear curve, not the harness's linear factor,
    so they match real devices. The linear renders here are a pessimistic
    bound.
- **1e. Window changes.** The class and scale are re-evaluated on every window
  size change: rotation, split-screen resize, fold/unfold. Destination,
  selection, scroll anchor and playback survive the change. Nothing restarts or
  re-tunes.
- **1f. Insets.** Handheld honors `SafeArea`, display cutouts and IME insets
  under edge-to-edge, unlike the TV margin rule. Backgrounds (the Guide bleed,
  the Player video) paint under the system bars; text and controls stay inside
  the safe area. The canvas already divides padding and insets by scale
  (`lineup_canvas.dart`); H1 keeps that.

**Why 0.9 rather than 1.0.** 1.0 meets touch size almost for free. But phone
canvases shrink to 360–412 units, headings already break at text 1.0 on a
360 dp phone, and body text is larger than Android's own body size. Making 0.9
work costs one rule (1c) on a short, named list of controls.
**Why not 0.8:** 14.4 dp body text and twice as many undersized targets, for
about 12 % more canvas width.

**Acceptance (H1).**
- `LineupCanvas.scaleFor` returns 0.9 for every Handheld window and the desktop
  rule for Wide windows, with widget tests at 360×800, 412×915, 915×412, 600×960,
  690×840, 800×1280, 960×600, 1280×800 and 1279×800 (Handheld) and 1280×719
  (Handheld).
- The semantics tap audit (the harness method) shows zero targets under 48 dp
  on accepted surfaces, except exemptions named in part 2.
- No overflow at text 1.3 and 2.0 for accepted surfaces in both classes.
- Rotation and resize tests keep destination, selection and playback.

## Item 2. Navigation and Back (MD-12)

**Problem.** Touch has no focus path, so a Back handler that walks the focused
node (today's `AndroidBackScope._systemBack`) can resolve against the wrong
surface after a tap. Destinations must also stay reachable without a keyboard.

**Rendered** (`sheets/item2-navigation.png`): the existing header with the
Lineup menu at 0.9 on phones, a portrait tablet (Handheld) and 1280×800 (Wide).
At 0.9 every menu row is 50 dp tall and passes the audit with no change.

**Recommendation.**

- **2a. Keep the desktop destination model on every handheld class:** the
  header (logo, hamburger for the Lineup menu, contextual Close) plus the Lineup
  menu with Guide, Player, Now Playing, Channels, Settings and Account.
  - No bottom navigation bar, navigation rail or drawer. Player and Now Playing
    are not peer tabs; a bar would cost Guide picture height on every screen.
  - The Lineup menu stays the anchored panel under the hamburger, as on desktop.
    It scrolls when the window is short.
  - Alternative considered: a bottom sheet for the menu. It's more
    thumb-reachable, but it detaches the menu from its invoker and diverges from
    desktop and TV for a five-row list. Not recommended for v1.
- **2b. System Back resolves the visible surface stack, not focus.** In order:
  1. an open anchored menu or dropdown (Lineup menu, Settings selects): close it;
  2. a dialog, picker or sheet (tracks, sleep, confirmations): close it, same
     as its Close/Cancel;
  3. surface states: in the Player, the Mini Guide closes, then expanded Now
     Playing collapses to the OSD. Back does not hide the OSD; tap does (item 4).
     The Guide's own states (an active search, an inspected program) slot in
     here; part 2 item 3 defines them;
  4. the destination: Player → Guide; Settings, Channels, Diagnostics → their
     origin (the existing "Back to …" target); onboarding sub-steps → the step
     their visible back link names (PIN → Profiles; linking → Cancel);
  5. nothing left (Guide root, welcome, or a step with no back link):
     background the task (`moveTaskToBack`). Playback stops per MD-8; on return,
     the current scheduled channel is retuned.
  - **General rule:** Back performs exactly the surface's visible back, close or
    cancel control. If there is none, it backgrounds the app.
  - Back never signs out, never discards a Setup draft without the existing
    confirmation, and never leaves audio playing.
  - Keyboards attached to tablets keep today's focus-based Esc behavior; only
    the OS Back gesture or button takes the surface-stack path.
  - While the soft keyboard is open, Android consumes the first Back to hide it
    before the app sees anything. The stack above starts from the next Back.
- **2c. Predictive back.** v1 keeps the root `PopScope(canPop: false)` claim, so
  Android shows no back-to-home preview and backgrounding has no system
  animation. In-app predictive animations are out of scope.

**Acceptance (H1).**
- A widget test per row of 2b on handheld, driven by the platform Back route
  request with focus placed elsewhere (for example on the Guide grid while a
  picker is open), asserting the surface that closes.
- A root Back test asserting `moveTaskToBack`.
- A test that Back on a PIN step returns to Profiles without signing out.
- Desktop and TV Back tests unchanged.

## Item 4R. Rotation and system bars (MD-3, MD-13)

**Rendered:** Player OSD at phone landscape 915×412 at 0.9
(`captures/h09-phone-915x412/player-osd--paused.png`). It fits as one row; the
portrait action row wraps at 0.9, which part 2 item 4 redesigns.

**Recommendation.**

- **4R-a. Phones, decided by the display's shortest side < 600 dp (not the
  window):** every surface is portrait-up only, except the Player.
  - The Player allows portrait-up and both landscapes, follows the sensor, and
    respects the system auto-rotate setting (with auto-rotate off it stays
    where it is).
  - Leaving the Player while in landscape returns to portrait for the
    destination it goes to.
  - Upside-down portrait is excluded.
- **4R-b. Tablets, foldable inner screens and any multi-window mode:** no
  orientation restriction anywhere. API 36 ignores locks there anyway; the app
  never asks.
- **4R-c. System bars outside the Player:** visible, transparent, light icons
  (the dark theme), content inside the safe area (1f).
- **4R-d. System bars in the Player (MD-13):** immersive-sticky (a swipe reveals
  the bars transiently) while the OSD is hidden, and always in landscape. In
  portrait with the OSD visible, the bars show. Leaving the Player restores
  4R-c. The adapter reports `fullscreen` truthfully (MD-18 `systemBars`).
- What the visible fullscreen control does on phones is part of part 2 item 4.

**Acceptance (H1 / H3N, V1).** `SystemChrome` orientation and UI-mode calls per
destination in widget tests (phone and tablet display sizes). Emulator rotation
on phone and tablet AVDs, with playback continuing across rotation. Immersive
entry and exit observed on the emulator.

## Item 7. Launcher icon, splash and system-bar theme (MD-17)

**Rendered** (`item7/`): `icon-masks.png` (today's legacy icon as Pixel shows it,
then the proposal under circle, squircle, rounded-square and teardrop masks,
then themed), `icon-safe-zone.png`, `home-and-splash.png` (real launcher size on
dark and light wallpapers; splash today and proposed). Built by
`ui/harness/icon.py` from `assets/branding/lineup-logo-mark.png`.

**Today.** One square PNG mipmap is shared by phone and TV
(`AndroidManifest.xml` `android:icon`). Phones show it shrunk inside a white
circle. `LaunchTheme` in light mode is `Theme.Light.NoTitleBar`, so a white window
flashes before the dark UI.

**Recommendation.**

- **7a. Phone icon: adaptive** (`mipmap-anydpi-v26/ic_launcher.xml`; minSdk 28 means
  every phone gets it). Layers on the 108 dp grid:
  - **background:** the logo tile's own diagonal lighting extended to full
    bleed (#4E5054 top-left to #1B1C1E bottom-right);
  - **foreground:** the logo's inner tile with the three cards, scaled so the
    cards are 50 dp tall, centered (+1 dp down), with a 9 dp feather into the
    background. The cards stay inside the 66 dp safe circle;
  - **monochrome** (Android 13+ themed icons): a vector of the three offset
    rounded cards at 43 %, 67 % and 100 % alpha, front card solid;
  - the existing PNG mipmaps stay as the legacy fallback.
  - The themed tint is expected to keep the layer's alpha, giving the three
    tones. M3b confirms this on the API 35/36 emulator. If the launcher flattens
    alpha, use the fallback: the front card solid and the two back cards as
    outlines.
  - Implementers export real layer assets at each density from the logo source,
    matching these specimens. The specimen PNGs are references, not shipping
    assets.
- **7b. TV icon unchanged, by construction.** Copy today's
  `mipmap-<density>/ic_launcher.png` to `mipmap-television-<density>/`. The
  `television` UI-mode qualifier outranks density and API level in Android's
  resource matching, so Google TV and Fire OS resolve today's exact files; phones
  resolve the adaptive icon. `tv_banner.png` is unchanged. **No TV icon approval
  is needed for this; verification is required (acceptance).**
- **7c. Splash and launch window:**
  - `LaunchTheme` and `NormalTheme` use a black no-title parent with
    `windowBackground` `#090806` (film gate) in both `values` and
    `values-night`, plus `windowSplashScreenBackground` `#090806` for API 31+.
  - The API 31+ splash icon is the launcher icon: adaptive on phones, today's
    TV icon on TV.
  - **TV-visible change:** the launch window on TV becomes `#090806` instead of
    the system's light or dark default. This removes a possible white flash.
    **This one needs your explicit TV approval.**
- **7d. System-bar theme:** as 4R-c and 4R-d (transparent, light icons). Three-button
  navigation keeps Android's own contrast scrim.

**Acceptance (M3b, V1).**
- Phone emulator (API 30, 35, 36): the home-screen icon matches `icon-masks.png`
  under the launcher's mask, the themed icon appears when themed icons are on,
  the splash matches `home-and-splash.png`, and there's no white frame at cold
  start.
- TV emulator (Google TV API 36) and Fire OS: the app list, settings icon and
  banner are byte-identical to the current build's, and the launch window is
  `#090806`.
- The physical pass checks the Fire TV app list once (plan §10.3).

## Part 2 direction (agreed by the maintainer, October 30)

**Why it changed.** The first part 2 renders fitted the desktop composition onto
phones. The maintainer judged them not releasable. A research pass looked at the
phone and iPad apps of Channels, YouTube TV (including the maintainer's phone
screenshots), Fubo, Philo, Frndly, Plex, Pluto TV, Xfinity, Spectrum and DIRECTV
(store screenshots; no sign-in). The direction below takes what fits Lineup's
product rather than copying any one app:
- Lineup channels have no network logos; they have a number, a name, and Plex
  artwork for every program.
- Personal lineups are small (about 5 to 40 channels).
- Everything is scheduled; there is no on-demand or DVR destination.
- The Cinema Continuity signature (the playing picture with the program-color
  bleed above the schedule) already matches Plex's phone Live TV pattern.

**Agreed decisions** (they define items 3, 4 and 6, and amend item 2 on handheld):

1. **Guide, pattern A, adapted to Lineup.** One channel per row:
   - a channel tile with the number and short name;
   - an on-now card with the program artwork thumbnail, title, time left and an
     amber progress line;
   - later programs continue to the right, and horizontal swipe moves every row
     through time together under a ruler;
   - when something is playing, the picture and its color bleed are pinned above
     the rows;
   - a program sheet carries the details.
   - Portrait tablets use the same rows and show two or three programs each.
     Windows that qualify for Wide (≥ 1280×720 dp), and the short-wide Handheld
     windows already rendering well at 0.9, keep the desktop Guide.
   - Rejected: the dense classic grid (B), which is built for very large
     lineups and is the worst for touch and readability; a separate On Now
     card view (C), which has no future schedule, while A's artwork carries
     most of its appeal.
2. **Navigation, amending part 1 item 2a on handheld only.**
   - Phones get a bottom tab bar: Guide, Channels, Settings.
   - Portrait tablets get a side navigation rail with the same destinations.
   - Desktop-composition windows keep the approved header and Lineup menu.
   - Item 2b (Back by visible surface stack) is unchanged. Switching tabs is
     not a Back step; Back from a tab's root returns to Guide, then backgrounds
     the app.
3. **Player in phone portrait.**
   - The video is pinned at the top.
   - Below it: the now-playing details, the Subtitles, Audio and Sleep chips,
     then channel rows. Tapping a row switches channels in place.
   - Rotating to landscape gives full screen with today's OSD (4R).
   - A visible minimize control (and a swipe down) shrinks the video to a mini
     player docked above the tab bar.
4. **Tap behavior, revising MD-4's "tap inspects" principle.**
   - Tapping an on-now program tunes that channel.
   - Tapping a later program, or long-pressing any program, opens the program
     sheet with a visible Watch action.
   - Every tune still has a visible equivalent.

**Unchanged from earlier in part 2:** Settings becomes a list that opens each
category as its own page on phones. Onboarding keeps its current single column;
phones hide the QR code and its "scan with your phone" line. Keyboard hints
are hidden on touch devices.

**Next:** build these in the specimen at the approved 0.9 scale, render
them for every handheld size with the touch and text-scale audits, and append
items 3, 4, 5 and 6 with exact rules for approval.

### Item 3: Guide (portrait composition)

- **Header:**
  - logo mark (34 cu) and "Guide" (Instrument Sans 30, which yields space when
    needed);
  - a "Now" text button that returns to live and resets the swipe;
  - "Filter by library" and "Search channels" icon buttons (54 cu). They open
    the existing library picker and search field as sheets; H2 renders those
    for review;
  - header text is capped at 1.3.
- **Pinned picture**, shown when a live picture exists:
  - 16:9 at full width, under the header. Tapping it opens the Player.
  - Under it, a now-strip with the program-color bleed (artwork color at 45 %
    over the primary surface), "N · Channel", the title (Instrument 24) and a
    progress bar.
  - With nothing playing, there is no picture and the rows start under the
    header.
- **Ruler:** "Today" in the tile column, labels every 30 minutes, and a coral
  now-line across the rows.
- **Rows:**
  - height 84 cu (phone) or 80 (tablet), times the text scale capped at 1.3;
  - row text is capped at 1.3 (a named clamp under rule 1d).
- **Channel tile:**
  - 100 cu wide (phone) or 120 (tablet);
  - number in Instrument 26, name at 12 cu on up to two lines;
  - the name wraps only between words; a word that cannot fit shrinks the
    text scale, never below 1.0;
  - tapping the tile tunes; the tuned channel uses the tuned-surface color.
- **Time scale:**
  - phone: 30 minutes is 55 % of the program width;
  - tablet: 75 minutes fills the program width;
  - origin is the controller's window start; a horizontal swipe moves every
    row and the ruler together, clamped to the window.
- **Program cards:**
  - surfaces: elevated for on-now, primary for later, selected for the tuned
    channel's on-now card; 8 cu radius; 6 cu gaps;
  - a 2:3 poster appears only on an on-now card ≥ 150 cu wide whose program
    has a poster;
  - title (show title first) at 17 cu, weight 500;
  - second line: on now, "27m left" or "● Watching · 43m left"; later, the
    start time;
  - a 3 cu amber progress line runs along the bottom of on-now cards;
  - text stays in view while a card's start scrolls off.
- **Tap:**
  - on-now card or channel tile: tunes;
  - later card: opens the program sheet;
  - long-press on any card: opens the program sheet.
- **Program sheet:**
  - a bottom modal, 20 cu top radius, scrolling, at most 90 % of the height;
  - a program-color gradient (artwork color at 50 % over the primary surface,
    fading into it), a drag handle, and a 104 cu poster;
  - "N · Channel", the title (Instrument 30), the episode, and either
    "start–end · Nm left" or "start–end · N min";
  - media badges and up to 6 lines of synopsis;
  - for a later program: "Airs at 11:00 PM. Watching tunes channel 2 now.";
  - actions: "Watch channel N" (filled) and Close.
- **States:**
  - a loading row shows "Loading schedule…";
  - a failed row shows "Schedule unavailable" with Retry;
  - when every row fails, a full overlay shows "Schedules couldn't load" with
    one Retry, and the rows stay quiet;
  - with no channels, the existing "No channels yet" / "Set up channels"
    state shows.
- **Not changed:** the controller, the window model, the scheduling.
